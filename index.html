HOW TO USE THIS PAGE

Real topographic maps of PUBG's four 8×8 km maps, built from the game's own terrain data. All heights are in metres.

  • Move around — drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Pick a map with the buttons at the top.
  • Read the ground — contour lines join points of equal height: thin lines every 10 m, labelled lines every 50 m. Lines packed close together mean steep ground; wide apart means flat.
  • Height colour — green is low ground, pink is high (the KEY at the bottom of the panel), on the same 0–250 m scale on every map. Blue is always water — the shoreline is drawn at −0.5 m, so the shallow edge counts as land.
  • The readout at the bottom left follows your cursor: grid square, position, and the elevation under the pointer — or the depth, under water.
  • Grid references — the letters and numbers around the edge name each 1 km square, like C4. Add the 100 m grid for finer references.
  • Fine detail — the small stuff: dips, ditches and rises of a few metres that can hide a prone player or break a line of sight.
  • Measure — tick Measure in the panel, click two points: distance, bearing and elevation change.
  • Two bases — the topo sheet, or the game's own map art with the topo layers drawn over it. Art opacity fades between them.
  • Export — downloads a square PNG of the current view, for sharing or printing.
  • Feedback — something missing or broken? Send feedback — every note gets read.

One honest limit: everything here comes from terrain height alone. Buildings and trees aren't in the data, so a slope that looks clear may still have a house on it.
